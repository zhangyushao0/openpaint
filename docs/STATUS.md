## Working features

- Opening (S54, D81, schema 23): an artwork opens complete in its first frame, nothing filling in, once its canvas was complete under this build: the app keeps each artwork's canvas as last shown complete (`cacheDir/canvas/<id>.bin`, the ten-bit pixels exactly, LZ4, about 8 MB for the author's A4 artwork) and shows it when it is what the artwork's records evaluate to (the save that wrote the artwork, this build's renderer, the canvas's format and size match). Otherwise (the first open under a new build, after an update, a library put back from a backup, a deleted or damaged cache) the cached canvas, or else the gallery picture enlarged, shows under the tiles while they fill in as before (8 s for the author's A4 artwork since S55), never paper; once complete it is written again, as after every save. Each save writes a new random save id into the artwork's head; an artwork without one (upgraded, imported, restored as a copy, new) gets one when it is opened. Deleting an artwork deletes its cached canvas. Exports, files, versions, picks and the snapshots under the pen never read it.
- Navigation never waits (S53, S54, D81): every frame shows at once what is held (the canvas, or a view tier enlarged) and gives the work still waiting at most 4 ms of the GPU's time (half a 120 Hz frame) while a finger or the pen touched within 0.5 s, 10 ms once nothing has (the author's A4 artwork fills in 8 s; 33 s at S54, 331 s at S53), so pinching, panning, taps and panels answer while a fresh start, a large change or a new tier fills in. What waits fills nearest the screen's middle first, then outwards, then what lies off screen; waiting tiles show what the canvas held before (a cached canvas or the gallery picture at opening, paper only on a new canvas), and those reading a smudge wait until its residual is evaluated, a share each frame, while the rest fills. Nothing fills while a stroke is drawn and the stroke never waits; zoomed in, where a stroke's tier tiles cost more than the frame has left, the enlarged canvas shows until they fill again after the lift. A pick, the gallery picture and exports still evaluate everything at once; layer pictures, the brush strip and brush previews evaluate in small steps, each waited for. A last artwork that needs repair (S50) starts the app in the gallery. Every 10 s while fingers navigate or work waits the app logs `navigation, …`: frames, frame work p50/p95/max, background GPU time a frame and in all, dispatches, entries, plans binned, residuals evaluated, tier tiles filled.
- Version, name and icon (S52, D79, no format change): Actions ends, under Pen, with a quiet grey line "OpenPaint 0.7.3 · schema 23" (nothing to tap; 0.7.0 at S52, a patch per slice). The APK is `dev.openpaint` `versionName` 0.7.3+schema23, `versionCode` 703, still debug-signed and debuggable; both come from the workspace manifest's version and the store's schema through `cargo android-build` (Gradle refuses a build without them). The launcher says "OpenPaint" under an adaptive icon: the paper (250, 248, 244) with the app's Brush icon (handle, ferrule, bristles to a point) drawn in one ink line (38, 37, 35), 2 of the icon's 108 units (1.5 points on the tablet's launcher, which shows the icon's circle 54 points wide), round caps and joins, inside the circle every mask keeps; a themed launcher tints that line (the monochrome layer); vector drawables only.
- Memory over long sessions (S51, D78, no format change): many smudges no longer freeze the app. Before, about 55 default-size smudges over one place filled the 128 MiB the tablet's GPU binds for their residuals and from then on every frame failed: canvas and interface stood still. Now an evaluation holds only the smudge residuals reaching what it evaluates, whole when the device binds them all, else just their tiles near it; the residuals needed least lately go first when the arena passes a quarter of the GPU budget (96 MiB on the tablet, 512 MiB on a GPU with its own memory), never one the frame needs, and come back from the strokes when a later frame needs them; an evaluation of everything waiting at once (a pick, the gallery picture) is cut back to the room right after; one layer alone (pictures, fills, lifts) is evaluated in bands of 512 rows. Once a minute while a canvas is open the app logs what holds memory (strokes, GPU caches, wgpu's allocations and blocks by label, the scene, plan and history on the heap), and the library logs fjall's cache and memtables at each new 4 MiB. On the tablet wgpu's allocator takes memory in blocks of 8 to 64 MiB (Graphics 108 MB with a blank canvas, was 174). Every pixel is as before. A place crossed by more smudges than fit whole paints and opens slowly (Known limits).
- Storage and session robustness (S50, D77, no format change): a stroke still being drawn when the app is left (home, the screen turned off, a call) or its window goes is committed as drawn so far, as its lift would commit it (every sample the app had, the line's end onto the pen, its mirrored copies, the end tapered), and saved before the app waits for the store; back in the app it is there at the zoom, place and angle left, one undo takes it back, and the pen's cancel that follows changes nothing (a pen held still 0.5 s before still snaps, QuickShape). Every write is one atomic batch: a crash, an install or a full disk in the middle of a save leaves the artwork as saved last, never half of a save; leaving writes the save, the gallery picture and the kept version each whole; a version that cannot be written is not kept (logged), the artwork unaffected; a failed save shows the notice with Retry as before. An update whose schema steps fail on one artwork (a record cut short or with an unknown tag, a step that panics) keeps that artwork's records exactly as they were and upgrades the others: its card's picture says "Needs repair" on paper at its foot, tapping it says "Needs repair: its records could not be upgraded." with OK, and its options still save it as an OpenPaint file of its own schema, which a build whose steps read it opens; a later build's upgrade tries it again.
- GPU robustness (S49, D76, no format change): no evaluation asks the GPU for more than about a tenth of a second at once: every evaluation (opening, undo and redo, layer, mask and merge changes, the stroke's snapshot tiles, smudge materials, view tiers, exports, layer pictures, the brush strip) goes in dispatches of at most 12,000 listed ops (each costed as a Textured Round span on the tablet, 33 ns a pixel), each submitted alone, and smudges in submissions of at most 2^24 nodes; on screen a large change fills in over frames within S53's frame budget. A lost GPU device (wgpu's lost callback, out of memory, a failed surface or GPU wait) is made again at the next frame: a new device and pipelines, the window's surface again from the window kept since attach, every UI texture uploaded again, the canvas and tiers evaluated anew; the artwork, history, toolbox, panels, view and the stroke being drawn stay (the stroke goes on under the pen and commits whole at the lift); a pill says "The display was reset." with OK until OK or the next stroke; at most three rebuilds a minute. A GPU memory budget per device class (384 MiB on a GPU sharing the system's memory, the tablet; 2 GiB on one with its own) covers the canvas, the view tiers, the snapshots, the smudge and filter arena with its material and the layer pictures; above it the tiers go first (the canvas shows magnified), then the snapshots (strokes walk every op beneath), then the arena's blocks no scene shows and its material, then the layer pictures (paused); each keeps within the room left, every drop logged, nothing fails. The app logs the time, frames and dispatches to the full canvas and each new peak of the caches.
- Brush sets (S47, D74, schema 22): the Brush, Smudge and Eraser pickers show under their heading Recent, Sketching, Inking, Painting and Texture as words in two columns (five need about 460 points at 18 points, the panel holds 304), the set shown in the ink pill, which slides to another tapped; a picker opens on the set holding its tool's brush, never on Recent. Under the words that set's brushes with their pictures, the tool's brush outlined wherever it lies (none while its set is not shown); a tap chooses, the chosen row tapped again opens its editor; StreamLine under them. At first: Sketching Pencil; Inking Ink Pen, Inker, Monoline; Painting Hard Round, Soft Brush, Flat, Opaque Paint; Texture Textured Round, Speckle; a copy lies right after its source. Recent: the brushes of the last six committed strokes of any tool (brush, eraser, smudge; not a cancelled stroke or one on the editor's strip), newest first, no repeats, never arranged; with none "Brushes you draw with appear here." in the grey hint. In the other four a finger or pen held 0.4 s on a row lifts it (paper and an ink outline, as a layer row), it follows the hand, the other rows slide to make room, and let go it lands there (where it lay: nothing, and no tap); the list does not scroll. The editor's Set row, under the name above the strip: "Set", then Sketching, Inking, Painting, Texture in two columns, the brush's in the pill; another tapped moves the brush to that set's end and Back shows that set. Sets, order and Recent are the toolbox's for every tool and artwork, reopen as left and are no undo steps; a stored key no brush has (a repeat, one past six) is dropped (logged), a brush in no stored set goes where a first start puts it (a copy whose source is gone: Painting).
- Brush editor (S46, D73, schema 21): in the Brush, Smudge or Eraser picker, a tap on the chosen brush's row again turns the panel into its editor in place: the name (tapped: a Rename brush dialog, Done or Enter renames, 1 to 40 characters) and Back at the top (under the name the Set row, S47); a paper-white strip the panel's width, 160 points high, showing an S-curve drawn with the brush at the tool's size and opacity in the current colour, pressure rising to full in the middle and falling, and every pen stroke drawn on it (Clear at its corner; fingers draw nothing; Back clears it), all redrawn with the brush as set after every change (on the brushes worker, a frame or two later); then one scrolling column (a finger drags it): Stroke (Spacing 0 to 4 of the size, 0 reading "Continuous"; StreamLine; Taper start and end 0 to 6 sizes; Taper tip), Shape (Disc, Square, Ellipse, Chalk, Grainy, Splatter, Rough square as words in two columns, the stamp in the ink pill, a continuous stroke's round tip as Disc; Aspect 5 to 100 %, grey for a disc; Rotation Fixed / Direction / Azimuth; Angle -180 to 180 degrees; Rotation jitter 0 to 180; Size jitter; Scatter 0 to 4 radii; Softness), Grain (Strength, Scale 1 to 64 px), Paint (Covering / Builds up; under Builds up Tip Flat / Bell, Flow 0.05 to 16, Max density, Paper None / Sketch), Size (Minimum, Maximum: the size slider's ends, 1 to 1000 px) and Dynamics (Size dynamics and Opacity dynamics, each a page of Pressure, Speed and Tilt squares with Reset and the axis's ends; Back to the settings); at the bottom Duplicate, and Reset for a built-in (grey while it is as its file) or Delete for a copy ("Delete <name>?", strokes drawn with it stay). Each slider is one row, its value under its name and in a bubble over the knob while held. Ranges follow the recipe so the engine never refuses one: a shape tapped on a continuous stroke lays stamps 0.1 sizes apart; spacing below half the least the stamps fit at turns continuous (the stamps come back while it is edited); scatter, size jitter, aspect and a size curve's points stop where a pixel would take more than 48 stamps; the paper greys the shapes and Bell (and is grey for stamps or a bell); Builds up greys Softness. Every change draws the next stroke of every tool on that brush; drawn strokes keep their look; nothing is an undo step. A copy is "<name> copy" right after its source, chosen, its own key; Delete chooses its source. The library (edited built-ins whole, copies, the order) is stored with the toolbox and reopens as left; a stored brush the engine refuses comes back as its built-in (logged). Changed brushes' row pictures are made again while the list shows.
- Shapes and scatter (S45, D72, schema 20): the brush panel lists ten brushes by job, Soft Brush followed by Textured Round, Flat, Opaque Paint and Speckle, each a row of stamps laid along the stroke and evaluated per pixel (sharp at every zoom and in exports, the same on every frame). Textured Round (size 40; size 0.35 + 0.65 p, opacity 0.25 + 0.75 p): the chalk texture every tenth of its size, turned up to 15 degrees either way at random, grain 0.3 in 5 px cells inside, covering: a chalky, broken edge, no dot rhythm. Flat (30; opacity 0.2 + 0.8 p): a square a quarter as long as wide every 0.05 of its size, held across the stroke and turning with it: one width, clean square ends, never darker where it crosses itself. Opaque Paint (40; opacity 0.3 + 0.7 p): the rough-square texture half as long as wide every 0.08, turning with the stroke, up to 4 degrees and 10 % of size at random: a solid band, a frayed, streaky edge and ragged ends. Speckle (12; opacity 0.3 + 0.7 p): discs every half its size, scattered up to 3 radii along and across, sized 1 ± 0.5, building up where they overlap (flow 2: a lone dot 86 %). On the tablet each stroke takes its own seed, so no two scatter alike; mirrored copies and a snapped shape keep their stroke's; undo, redo and reopening give it back exactly. A tap leaves one stamp. Every stroke drawn before draws as it did. Brush files may hold in `[shape]` `stamp` (disc, square, ellipse, chalk, grainy, splatter, rough-square), `aspect`, `spacing`, `scatter`, `rotation` (fixed, direction, azimuth), `angle`, `rotation_jitter`, `size_jitter`.
- Taper and tilt (S44, D71, schema 19): each brush row's picture is painted by the evaluator. Inker: the Ink Pen's pressure width narrowing at both ends to a tenth of its radius, over 2 diameters of its size at the start and 3 at the end (smoothstep along the path); while the pen is down the line grows from its point and its end under the pen is blunt; the frame after the lift narrows the end, and that is the stroke stored (undo, redo and reopen give it exactly). A dot (one sample) is never tapered; a stroke shorter than both tapers stays thinner than full width all along; the eraser tapers too, a smudge only at its start; the pen's outline shows the brush untapered. Pencil: unchanged up to 60 degrees of tilt (how a pen is held: the author's leans 45 at the median), then wider and lighter, at 75 degrees three times as wide with a quarter of the graphite. Monoline: one width at any pressure, full strength, StreamLine 60 % at first (every other brush 0 %). Hard Round: size 0.25 + 0.75 p and opacity 0.1 + 0.9 p of the stroke's, a hard covering edge. Ink Pen and Soft Brush draw as before; every stroke drawn before keeps its look (its recipe snapshot gains flat tilt curves and no taper). Brush files may hold `tilt` curves in `[size]` and `[opacity]` (degrees 0 to 90; a size up to 4 times), a `[taper]` (start, end, tip) and a top-level `streamline`.
- QuickShape (S42, D69, no format change): a stroke (brush, eraser or smudge) whose pen rests within 5 points for 0.5 s (by the input clock; frames keep coming while the pen is down) snaps to the shape it resembles: a line from where it began to where the pen rests, a circle's arc, an ellipse (a circle when its short axis is at least 0.9 of its long one), or a polyline of 2 to 6 straight sides with sharp corners (open, or closed as a triangle, rectangle, polygon); a stroke resembling none, or under 30 points long, stays as drawn (one try per rest). Still pressing, moving the pen turns and sizes the shape about its start (open) or middle (closed). The stored stroke is samples along the exact shape (at most 16 px and 5 degrees apart, and 0.5 and 2 px either side of each corner, so the curve through them keeps corners sharp), each carrying the pressure, tilt and time the drawn stroke had at the same share of its length (the azimuth of the nearer sample). After the lift a pill "Edit shape" in the top band's middle; tapped, the handles show (a line's ends, an arc's ends and middle, an ellipse's four axis ends: the axis turns to the hand, the other keeps its length; a polyline's corners; pen or finger, the Transform tool's marks) and the pill says Done; every drag replaces the stroke (and its copies) in the same history step, so one undo goes back to before the stroke; Done or any other intent but navigation and saving ends it. Recognition (`quickshape_fit.rs`): the path at 96 even points, the resting samples dropped; closed when its end comes within 0.2 of the size (bounding diagonal) of its start, cut where nearest, enclosing at least 0.03 size²; a line when no point strays 0.05 size from the chord and the path is at most 1.15 chords; else the closer fit (root mean square) of arc (Kasa least squares, turning 25 to 330 degrees one way) or ellipse (the enclosed area's moments) and polyline (Douglas-Peucker at 0.035 size, corners turning at least 30 degrees, sides at least 0.08 size, each side the least-squares line through its middle 70 %, corners where sides meet, the path passing within 0.03 size of each), a fit counting within 0.025 size on average and 0.08 at worst.
- Drawing Guide (S42, D69): Actions > Canvas ends with a Drawing Guide switch and Vertical, Horizontal, Quadrant, Radial as words in two columns (the one in use in the ink pill while on; a word tapped turns it on with that symmetry), and while on the hint "A finger moves the centre."; over the canvas faint lines through the centre (upright, level, both, or four 45 degrees apart) and a faint handle a finger drags (the pen never: a stroke begun at the centre draws). Every stroke (brush, eraser, smudge, QuickShape too) is drawn again mirrored (Procreate's default, not rotational: 1, 1, 3 or 7 copies, the radial ones the square's eight symmetries), live while drawn (the canvas shows the artwork with the copies under the stroke), each copy a stroke record with the stroke's style and selection, committed with it as one undo step (copies first, the stroke last). Fills, selections, transforms and adjustments are not mirrored. The guide is session state: every artwork opens with it off, and it is not stored.
- Pen buttons (S41, S43, D70, schema 18): Actions ends with a Pen section, Pen buttons with what the side buttons do as its note ("Eraser · Eyedropper · Undo" at first); tapped, the panel shows its page in place (and again when Actions reopens, until Back or the panel closes): Back, "For the buttons your pen has.", Side button 1, 2 and 3 (from the tip outwards: Android's `BUTTON_STYLUS_PRIMARY`, `BUTTON_STYLUS_SECONDARY`, `BUTTON_TERTIARY`) each with Nothing, Eraser, Eyedropper, Undo, Redo, Switch tool as words in two columns (the one chosen in the ink pill, sliding), then Eraser end, "Erases with the eraser's brush." Eraser and Eyedropper act while held: from the pen's next contact (hovering or touching down with it pressed) the pen erases with the eraser's brush (the Eraser button carries the ring, the hover outline is the eraser's) or picks (the loupe, the colour on each lift, every contact while held); released, the tool chosen before is back (released on the glass: after the lift). Undo, Redo and Switch tool (eraser from any tool, brush from the eraser) act once per press, hovering or touching (on the glass: at the lift); pressed together they act from the tip outwards. The eraser end erases with the eraser's brush from the moment it comes into range until it lifts or leaves, whatever the buttons do. The first button holding a tool wins, then the second, then the third. Held tools are never stored; the chosen map and tool are, with the toolbox (a schema-17 toolbox opens with the third on Undo).
- Palette and hex (S41, D70): the colour panel's header holds a field showing the colour as #RRGGBB; tapped, its text is selected and the keyboard comes up; Enter (the keyboard's Done) with six hex digits (# optional, either case) makes that the colour (and a recent one); anything else shows "Type a colour as #RRGGBB." and keeps the colour; leaving the field shows the colour again. - Copy and paste (S40, D67, schema 16): three fingers swiped down on the canvas (their middle at least 48 points down, within 30 degrees of vertical, by 0.8 s) or Actions > Copy & Paste ("Three fingers down") open a pill in the top band's middle (under the islands while the selection or transform bar shows): Cut, Copy, Copy All, Duplicate, Cut & Paste, Paste, pencil grey where there is nothing for them; a command or a tap on the canvas outside closes it; a three-finger tap still redoes, two fingers still navigate. They act on the selection, or on the whole active layer with none (Copy All: on what every visible layer shows, without the paper). Copy and Copy All hold records (the layer's strokes and edits as a nested record at full opacity by Normal through its mask; Copy All a record per clipping chain and faded group), the pixels outside a selection cleared, for the app's run only (not stored, not the system clipboard). Paste puts them on a new layer "Pasted layer" above the active one, framed by the Transform tool at once; moved or not, Transform (or any other edit) commits layer and place as one step, undo at the first step takes the paste back, a committed paste deselects. Cut clears the selection's pixels (or empties the layer's paint), one step; Duplicate and Cut & Paste are one step each. Paste is grey with nothing copied or a copy from a canvas of another size; a copy of nothing says "Nothing to paste: the copy holds no paint." with OK. Everything pasted and cut reopens as left.
- Merge Down (S39, D66, schema 15): the layer footer holds, after Add Mask, an arrow onto a line and the words Merge Down. Tapped, the chosen layer goes into the one below as a nested record (its strokes, edits, mask, opacity, mode and whether it clipped, nothing baked); the lower row stays, chosen, with its name, place and switches; the canvas does not change: a layer clipped to the one below merges bit for bit at any mode, opacity and masks; two layers of one mode (Normal, Multiply, Screen) merge with at most one 8-bit level in rare pixels, the lower layer's enabled mask (and its opacity below 100 %) moving into a record of its own, so the merged row then shows 100 % and no mask. Elsewhere Merge Down is pencil grey with why in quieter small text at its row's end: Nothing below (bottom of the stack or of a group), Hidden layer, Locked layer, Modes clash (different modes, or Overlay, without clipping), Below clips, Has clips, Too deep (8 levels). Strokes, fills, adjustments, filters, smudges and transforms after a merge act on the whole merged layer; a merged layer merges again; duplicate copies it with new ids; one undo step; reopens, exports at 1x/2x/4x, versions and OpenPaint files carry it; the row's picture shows everything. While the mask row is chosen the footer shows the mask's options (choose the layer's row to merge).

- Ten-bit display (S37, D64, no format change): on the tablet the canvas the screen shows (at and below about 1.4 screen pixels per canvas pixel) and the sharp-zoom tiers above it hold 10 bits per channel and the window presents Rgb10a2Unorm in sRGB, which the display processor composites as it is; soft brushes, low opacities, blending and the bilinear zoom show four times finer steps (each a ten-bit code within one code of the exact value). Picking, ColorDrop, thumbnails, transform lifts, exports (8-bit sRGB), files and every canvas hash are exactly as before (each code reads back as the 8-bit value an 8-bit canvas holds). A device without ten-bit compute writes keeps 8 bits throughout. Debug builds only: `cargo device-input gradient on` covers the whole screen with a grey ramp from sRGB 100 (left) to 120 (right), its upper half rounded to 8 bits (about 21 bands, 137 px wide), its lower half at 10 bits (about 80 bands, 36 px); `gradient off` ends it.
- Export at a scale (S29, D55): Actions > Share opens with the words 1×, 2×, 4× (1× in the ink pill at every start; the pill slides) and the file's size in quieter grey (2880 × 1716 at 1× on the tablet's screen-size canvas). Save PNG… and Share PNG… write at the chosen scale; at 4× Save JPEG… and Share JPEG… are pencil grey with "Up to 2×" and taps go nowhere. ## Known limits

- Painting speed (S48, D75, S55, D82): a stroke over a place painted on before costs what it does over an empty layer; the frames left above a 120 Hz vsync are the pen-down and the lift, which rebuild the whole scene (on the tablet 18 to 20 ms at 300 Textured Round strokes, 25 to 37 ms at 600; injected strokes of 52 samples, the author's average about 340, so his real strokes cost several times as much each; ROADMAP Candidates). Opaque Paint at 400 px over 300 strokes (S55): the frame after the pen-down lays the previous stroke into the snapshot over the first frame's region (60 to 73 ms), the pen-down's rebuild takes 26 to 34 ms, the lift evaluates the last spans over every op beneath (frame work p95 of 52-sample strokes 18.6 ms); the first stroke after opening takes the snapshot's tiles (1.1 s at 400 px). Strokes submitted faster than the GPU evaluates them queue: 60 injected Textured Round strokes at the author's large size made the lifts wait 2.5 to 11 s until Android killed the app. On the Adreno 735 every op kind still costs 40 to 130 times its RTX 5070 time per pixel (one register file for every kind: code added for one kind slows them all, measured with `device-input bench` before it is kept); the benchmark runs on the app's main thread, frames stop meanwhile. The first stroke over a place after opening the artwork, an undo or a layer switch takes each tile once, as costly as a whole frame was before S48 (a few tiles a frame; a frame of the first stroke over 600 such strokes costs the dev GPU 1.2 to 3 ms against 0.06 for the next). A stroke on a mask walks its layer's paint; zoomed in past 1.4 (a view tier) a stroke evaluates every op beneath it as before; other layers are binned and evaluated as before. The snapshot takes 16 bytes per pixel of the tiles painted over plus 0.3 MB (at most 79 MB at screen size) while its layer is painted on, within the device's storage binding limit and the room the GPU memory budget leaves it (past either a frame evaluates every op).
- Display cache (S54, D81): an artwork shows complete at once only from the start after a fill under this build completed: the first open after every update (a new version or shader text), after a library put back from a backup, after the system cleared the app's cache, and of an artwork never opened since S54 fills once (8.2 to 8.5 s for the author's A4 artwork since S55) with the cached canvas or the gallery picture under the filling tiles; the file is read on the storage worker before the canvas opens (27 to 36 ms) and uploaded on the main thread (17 to 20 ms for 35 MB); after every save whose canvas is then complete the whole canvas is read back and written again (35 MB, 0.1 s on a worker, one at a time); a cached canvas evaluates its smudge residuals in the background after opening (seconds), and a stroke near a smudge before then evaluates them in its first frame; a project id a library restore reused may show another artwork's cached canvas under its filling tiles (never as complete: the save id differs).
- Frame budget and GPU robustness (S49, S53, S54, D81): background work gets 4 ms of GPU a frame while the screen is touched, 10 ms otherwise (fills: the busiest artwork's fresh start 8.2 to 8.5 s, a 2x tier of 165 tiles 8.5 s, a 4x tier 11.4 s; S54 32.7 s, 24.8 and 28.6 s for 117 tiles; S53's 4 ms alone 149 to 361, 124 and 229 s), the screen showing the enlarged canvas or what the canvas held meanwhile; frames run at about 70 Hz while it fills untouched. One piece above the budget still runs whole: a smudge's material tile over the busiest place takes 9 to 33 ms of GPU, so while a fresh start evaluates residuals frames run p95 17 to 27 ms (2 of 14 and 9 of 36 10-s windows in the two runs); smudge strokes over the busiest place while a tier fills ran p95 22 to 49 ms (9 to 12 with the tier complete). A single 16-px tile listing more than 12,000 ops is one dispatch; exports and filter kernels are not paced; the cost table comes from S55's benchmark (its estimate 0.3 to 0.5 of the time where tiles hold one to eight plain ops) and `PARALLEL` from one artwork on the tablet. Making the renderer again takes about 3 s on the tablet (every pipeline compiled anew), and a job running on a worker at the loss (an export, a picture) fails with its notice; the loss was tried on the tablet only by destroying the device on purpose. The painter keeps a CPU copy of every UI texture (the font atlas, gallery pictures, the reference image) for a rebuild. The budget's figures are bytes the app allocates (textures, buffers), not the driver's own; the window's buffers and the UI's textures are outside it. ROADMAP Candidates.
- Development GPU (found in S48): on the RTX 5070 (Vulkan, driver 617.14) a cold evaluation of stamped textures comes out in one of two results a level apart in a few hundred channels, switching every second or so in a busy process (at HEAD too; plain round strokes never; two runs of 80 evaluations flipped at the same rounds); so S48's tests compare plain content bit for bit and textured content within that level, and other bit-exact GPU tests over stamped content can fail now and then (none has).
- Adjustments (S28, S33): no Pencil mode, no colour balance, gradient map, noise, motion or perspective blur; a curve point cannot be removed (Reset clears the curve shown); curves are tables of 8-bit entries read linearly between them (within 1 level of the exact curve on random inputs, tested; the composite curve applies to each channel's rounded table entry); previews (S35, D75): a slider's first value evaluates the layer in full and takes its snapshot (130 ms on the test artwork); layers under and over the adjusted one are evaluated again wherever its content lies each value (a busy layer below costs every value); zoomed in past 1.4 (a view tier) each value evaluates the tier's tiles in full; the snapshot (16 bytes per pixel of the content's tiles, up to 79 MB) stays until the next edit; on a mask or under a transform record an unclipped adjustment still reaches the whole canvas and resumes from no snapshot; the open panel covers the side bar's lower half; on paint `inverted` is stored but has no meaning.
- Blur and sharpen (S33, S35): previews reach about 30 values a second on the test artwork at every radius, sharpen 33 (above the 60 Hz frame: the kernel about 9 to 13 ms and the evaluation of the tiles near the content 15 to 20 ms on the GPU, the evaluator costing about 2.5 µs a tile; D47, `fail`, ROADMAP Candidates); blurs from 4 px are within 3 levels of the exact Gaussian, not it; Alpha Lock is ignored (the blur spreads alpha); content a transform record brought in from beyond the canvas is not filtered (the material holds the canvas only); the result is 8 bits per channel at canvas resolution, enlarged bilinearly at 2x and 4x and in the zoom tiers (a 1-pixel sharpen halo enlarges with it); filter results always stay in the arena (only smudge residuals are dropped and made again, S51), within the device's storage binding limit (128 MiB on the tablet; `Admission` beyond); a preview's earlier values give their block back.
- Colour (S27): artworks are sRGB only (P3 is the author's decision below); PNG `gAMA`/`cHRM`/`cICP` without `iCCP` are not read, grey ICC profiles and CMYK JPEGs' profiles are taken as sRGB; a 16-bit PNG is reduced to 8 bits before its conversion; out-of-gamut colours clip per channel (hue can shift slightly for very saturated P3 colours); converting costs one extra pass over the picture on the transfer worker (not timed); the system picker opens in the last folder used (on the tablet the author's OneDrive), so device routines go through its menu to Downloads.
- Insert (S26): one picture per pick, PNG and JPEG only; a picture larger than the canvas was reduced once when inserted (later transforms are records over its pixels, sampled once through all of them); a picture the canvas's height (a portrait photo on a landscape canvas) puts the frame's turn handle under the top band's bar (pinch the view out to reach it, or use Rotate 45°); decoding takes the whole file in memory (up to 50 megapixels, about 200 MB); an inserted picture costs its RGBA bytes in history (64 MiB budget) like a transform's patch; the refusal for a layer in a hidden group reads "Can't transform: the layer is hidden.".
- Masks (S24): one per layer, none on groups; the mask's grey is the current colour's luma (no grey-only colour panel), so black and white are chosen on the wheel or from recents; a mask does not move with a whole-layer transform and cannot be transformed itself; no apply-mask (Merge Down applies a lower layer's enabled mask inside its nested record); an off mask's row cannot be chosen (its tap does nothing until Turn mask on); turning a mask on, off or inverting it re-evaluates the whole canvas; the footer grew by the Mask row (8 rows, 10 in a group); the eraser reveals (white) where draweave's hid.

- Smudge (S21): large sizes are slow (above about 340 px frame work passes 16.7 ms at p95; 600 px runs at about 25 to 35 Hz); every contact runs two dispatches over its (2r + 3)^2 nodes, about 16 contacts a frame at a fast pen, and the footprint is evaluated again over everything beneath. It drags only the active layer. A residual costs 20 bytes per pixel of its 32-pixel tiles on the GPU (a long 120 px smudge about 5 to 10 MB; a default-size injected one of 600 px about 1.5 MB); since S51 they are a cache within a quarter of the GPU budget (D78, Known limits below).

## Working features

- Storage and session robustness (S50, D77, no format change): a stroke still being drawn when the app is left (home, the screen turned off, a call) or its window goes is committed as drawn so far, as its lift would commit it (every sample the app had, the line's end onto the pen, its mirrored copies, the end tapered), and saved before the app waits for the store; back in the app it is there at the zoom, place and angle left, one undo takes it back, and the pen's cancel that follows changes nothing (a pen held still 0.5 s before still snaps, QuickShape). Every write is one atomic batch: a crash, an install or a full disk in the middle of a save leaves the artwork as saved last, never half of a save; leaving writes the save, the gallery picture and the kept version each whole; a version that cannot be written is not kept (logged), the artwork unaffected; a failed save shows the notice with Retry as before. An update whose schema steps fail on one artwork (a record cut short or with an unknown tag, a step that panics) keeps that artwork's records exactly as they were and upgrades the others: its card's picture says "Needs repair" on paper at its foot, tapping it says "Needs repair: its records could not be upgraded." with OK, and its options still save it as an OpenPaint file of its own schema, which a build whose steps read it opens; a later build's upgrade tries it again.
- GPU robustness (S49, D76, no format change): no evaluation asks the GPU for more than about a tenth of a second at once: every evaluation (opening, undo and redo, layer, mask and merge changes, the stroke's snapshot tiles, smudge materials, view tiers, exports, layer pictures, the brush strip) goes in dispatches of at most 12,000 listed ops (each costed as a Textured Round span on the tablet, 33 ns a pixel), each submitted alone, and smudges in submissions of at most 2^24 nodes. A large change on screen fills in over frames, two dispatches a frame, whole 16-px tiles from the top (on a new canvas the tiles still waiting show paper), while taps, panels and navigation answer; the stroke being drawn never waits; a pick or the gallery picture evaluates what waits first; zoomed in, the view tier fills only once the canvas is complete. A lost GPU device (wgpu's lost callback, out of memory, a failed surface or GPU wait) is made again at the next frame: a new device and pipelines, the window's surface again from the window kept since attach, every UI texture uploaded again, the canvas and tiers evaluated anew; the artwork, history, toolbox, panels, view and the stroke being drawn stay (the stroke goes on under the pen and commits whole at the lift); a pill says "The display was reset." with OK until OK or the next stroke; at most three rebuilds a minute. A GPU memory budget per device class (384 MiB on a GPU sharing the system's memory, the tablet; 2 GiB on one with its own) covers the canvas, the view tiers, the snapshots, the smudge and filter arena with its material and the layer pictures; above it the tiers go first (the canvas shows magnified), then the snapshots (strokes walk every op beneath), then the arena's blocks no scene shows and its material, then the layer pictures (paused); each keeps within the room left, every drop logged, nothing fails. The app logs the time, frames and dispatches to the full canvas and each new peak of the caches.
- Brush sets (S47, D74, schema 22): the Brush, Smudge and Eraser pickers show under their heading Recent, Sketching, Inking, Painting and Texture as words in two columns (five need about 460 points at 18 points, the panel holds 304), the set shown in the ink pill, which slides to another tapped; a picker opens on the set holding its tool's brush, never on Recent. Under the words that set's brushes with their pictures, the tool's brush outlined wherever it lies (none while its set is not shown); a tap chooses, the chosen row tapped again opens its editor; StreamLine under them. At first: Sketching Pencil; Inking Ink Pen, Inker, Monoline; Painting Hard Round, Soft Brush, Flat, Opaque Paint; Texture Textured Round, Speckle; a copy lies right after its source. Recent: the brushes of the last six committed strokes of any tool (brush, eraser, smudge; not a cancelled stroke or one on the editor's strip), newest first, no repeats, never arranged; with none "Brushes you draw with appear here." in the grey hint. In the other four a finger or pen held 0.4 s on a row lifts it (paper and an ink outline, as a layer row), it follows the hand, the other rows slide to make room, and let go it lands there (where it lay: nothing, and no tap); the list does not scroll. The editor's Set row, under the name above the strip: "Set", then Sketching, Inking, Painting, Texture in two columns, the brush's in the pill; another tapped moves the brush to that set's end and Back shows that set. Sets, order and Recent are the toolbox's for every tool and artwork, reopen as left and are no undo steps; a stored key no brush has (a repeat, one past six) is dropped (logged), a brush in no stored set goes where a first start puts it (a copy whose source is gone: Painting).
- Brush editor (S46, D73, schema 21): in the Brush, Smudge or Eraser picker, a tap on the chosen brush's row again turns the panel into its editor in place: the name (tapped: a Rename brush dialog, Done or Enter renames, 1 to 40 characters) and Back at the top (under the name the Set row, S47); a paper-white strip the panel's width, 160 points high, showing an S-curve drawn with the brush at the tool's size and opacity in the current colour, pressure rising to full in the middle and falling, and every pen stroke drawn on it (Clear at its corner; fingers draw nothing; Back clears it), all redrawn with the brush as set after every change (on the brushes worker, a frame or two later); then one scrolling column (a finger drags it): Stroke (Spacing 0 to 4 of the size, 0 reading "Continuous"; StreamLine; Taper start and end 0 to 6 sizes; Taper tip), Shape (Disc, Square, Ellipse, Chalk, Grainy, Splatter, Rough square as words in two columns, the stamp in the ink pill, a continuous stroke's round tip as Disc; Aspect 5 to 100 %, grey for a disc; Rotation Fixed / Direction / Azimuth; Angle -180 to 180 degrees; Rotation jitter 0 to 180; Size jitter; Scatter 0 to 4 radii; Softness), Grain (Strength, Scale 1 to 64 px), Paint (Covering / Builds up; under Builds up Tip Flat / Bell, Flow 0.05 to 16, Max density, Paper None / Sketch), Size (Minimum, Maximum: the size slider's ends, 1 to 1000 px) and Dynamics (Size dynamics and Opacity dynamics, each a page of Pressure, Speed and Tilt squares with Reset and the axis's ends; Back to the settings); at the bottom Duplicate, and Reset for a built-in (grey while it is as its file) or Delete for a copy ("Delete <name>?", strokes drawn with it stay). Each slider is one row, its value under its name and in a bubble over the knob while held. Ranges follow the recipe so the engine never refuses one: a shape tapped on a continuous stroke lays stamps 0.1 sizes apart; spacing below half the least the stamps fit at turns continuous (the stamps come back while it is edited); scatter, size jitter, aspect and a size curve's points stop where a pixel would take more than 48 stamps; the paper greys the shapes and Bell (and is grey for stamps or a bell); Builds up greys Softness. Every change draws the next stroke of every tool on that brush; drawn strokes keep their look; nothing is an undo step. A copy is "<name> copy" right after its source, chosen, its own key; Delete chooses its source. The library (edited built-ins whole, copies, the order) is stored with the toolbox and reopens as left; a stored brush the engine refuses comes back as its built-in (logged). Changed brushes' row pictures are made again while the list shows.
- Shapes and scatter (S45, D72, schema 20): the brush panel lists ten brushes by job, Soft Brush followed by Textured Round, Flat, Opaque Paint and Speckle, each a row of stamps laid along the stroke and evaluated per pixel (sharp at every zoom and in exports, the same on every frame). Textured Round (size 40; size 0.35 + 0.65 p, opacity 0.25 + 0.75 p): the chalk texture every tenth of its size, turned up to 15 degrees either way at random, grain 0.3 in 5 px cells inside, covering: a chalky, broken edge, no dot rhythm. Flat (30; opacity 0.2 + 0.8 p): a square a quarter as long as wide every 0.05 of its size, held across the stroke and turning with it: one width, clean square ends, never darker where it crosses itself. Opaque Paint (40; opacity 0.3 + 0.7 p): the rough-square texture half as long as wide every 0.08, turning with the stroke, up to 4 degrees and 10 % of size at random: a solid band, a frayed, streaky edge and ragged ends. Speckle (12; opacity 0.3 + 0.7 p): discs every half its size, scattered up to 3 radii along and across, sized 1 ± 0.5, building up where they overlap (flow 2: a lone dot 86 %). On the tablet each stroke takes its own seed, so no two scatter alike; mirrored copies and a snapped shape keep their stroke's; undo, redo and reopening give it back exactly. A tap leaves one stamp. Every stroke drawn before draws as it did. Brush files may hold in `[shape]` `stamp` (disc, square, ellipse, chalk, grainy, splatter, rough-square), `aspect`, `spacing`, `scatter`, `rotation` (fixed, direction, azimuth), `angle`, `rotation_jitter`, `size_jitter`.
- Taper and tilt (S44, D71, schema 19): each brush row's picture is painted by the evaluator. Inker: the Ink Pen's pressure width narrowing at both ends to a tenth of its radius, over 2 diameters of its size at the start and 3 at the end (smoothstep along the path); while the pen is down the line grows from its point and its end under the pen is blunt; the frame after the lift narrows the end, and that is the stroke stored (undo, redo and reopen give it exactly). A dot (one sample) is never tapered; a stroke shorter than both tapers stays thinner than full width all along; the eraser tapers too, a smudge only at its start; the pen's outline shows the brush untapered. Pencil: unchanged up to 60 degrees of tilt (how a pen is held: the author's leans 45 at the median), then wider and lighter, at 75 degrees three times as wide with a quarter of the graphite. Monoline: one width at any pressure, full strength, StreamLine 60 % at first (every other brush 0 %). Hard Round: size 0.25 + 0.75 p and opacity 0.1 + 0.9 p of the stroke's, a hard covering edge. Ink Pen and Soft Brush draw as before; every stroke drawn before keeps its look (its recipe snapshot gains flat tilt curves and no taper). Brush files may hold `tilt` curves in `[size]` and `[opacity]` (degrees 0 to 90; a size up to 4 times), a `[taper]` (start, end, tip) and a top-level `streamline`.
- QuickShape (S42, D69, no format change): a stroke (brush, eraser or smudge) whose pen rests within 5 points for 0.5 s (by the input clock; frames keep coming while the pen is down) snaps to the shape it resembles: a line from where it began to where the pen rests, a circle's arc, an ellipse (a circle when its short axis is at least 0.9 of its long one), or a polyline of 2 to 6 straight sides with sharp corners (open, or closed as a triangle, rectangle, polygon); a stroke resembling none, or under 30 points long, stays as drawn (one try per rest). Still pressing, moving the pen turns and sizes the shape about its start (open) or middle (closed). The stored stroke is samples along the exact shape (at most 16 px and 5 degrees apart, and 0.5 and 2 px either side of each corner, so the curve through them keeps corners sharp), each carrying the pressure, tilt and time the drawn stroke had at the same share of its length (the azimuth of the nearer sample). After the lift a pill "Edit shape" in the top band's middle; tapped, the handles show (a line's ends, an arc's ends and middle, an ellipse's four axis ends: the axis turns to the hand, the other keeps its length; a polyline's corners; pen or finger, the Transform tool's marks) and the pill says Done; every drag replaces the stroke (and its copies) in the same history step, so one undo goes back to before the stroke; Done or any other intent but navigation and saving ends it. Recognition (`quickshape_fit.rs`): the path at 96 even points, the resting samples dropped; closed when its end comes within 0.2 of the size (bounding diagonal) of its start, cut where nearest, enclosing at least 0.03 size²; a line when no point strays 0.05 size from the chord and the path is at most 1.15 chords; else the closer fit (root mean square) of arc (Kasa least squares, turning 25 to 330 degrees one way) or ellipse (the enclosed area's moments) and polyline (Douglas-Peucker at 0.035 size, corners turning at least 30 degrees, sides at least 0.08 size, each side the least-squares line through its middle 70 %, corners where sides meet, the path passing within 0.03 size of each), a fit counting within 0.025 size on average and 0.08 at worst.
- Drawing Guide (S42, D69): Actions > Canvas ends with a Drawing Guide switch and Vertical, Horizontal, Quadrant, Radial as words in two columns (the one in use in the ink pill while on; a word tapped turns it on with that symmetry), and while on the hint "A finger moves the centre."; over the canvas faint lines through the centre (upright, level, both, or four 45 degrees apart) and a faint handle a finger drags (the pen never: a stroke begun at the centre draws). Every stroke (brush, eraser, smudge, QuickShape too) is drawn again mirrored (Procreate's default, not rotational: 1, 1, 3 or 7 copies, the radial ones the square's eight symmetries), live while drawn (the canvas shows the artwork with the copies under the stroke), each copy a stroke record with the stroke's style and selection, committed with it as one undo step (copies first, the stroke last). Fills, selections, transforms and adjustments are not mirrored. The guide is session state: every artwork opens with it off, and it is not stored.
- Pen buttons (S41, S43, D70, schema 18): Actions ends with a Pen section, Pen buttons with what the side buttons do as its note ("Eraser · Eyedropper · Undo" at first); tapped, the panel shows its page in place (and again when Actions reopens, until Back or the panel closes): Back, "For the buttons your pen has.", Side button 1, 2 and 3 (from the tip outwards: Android's `BUTTON_STYLUS_PRIMARY`, `BUTTON_STYLUS_SECONDARY`, `BUTTON_TERTIARY`) each with Nothing, Eraser, Eyedropper, Undo, Redo, Switch tool as words in two columns (the one chosen in the ink pill, sliding), then Eraser end, "Erases with the eraser's brush." Eraser and Eyedropper act while held: from the pen's next contact (hovering or touching down with it pressed) the pen erases with the eraser's brush (the Eraser button carries the ring, the hover outline is the eraser's) or picks (the loupe, the colour on each lift, every contact while held); released, the tool chosen before is back (released on the glass: after the lift). Undo, Redo and Switch tool (eraser from any tool, brush from the eraser) act once per press, hovering or touching (on the glass: at the lift); pressed together they act from the tip outwards. The eraser end erases with the eraser's brush from the moment it comes into range until it lifts or leaves, whatever the buttons do. The first button holding a tool wins, then the second, then the third. Held tools are never stored; the chosen map and tool are, with the toolbox (a schema-17 toolbox opens with the third on Undo).
- Palette and hex (S41, D70): the colour panel's header holds a field showing the colour as #RRGGBB; tapped, its text is selected and the keyboard comes up; Enter (the keyboard's Done) with six hex digits (# optional, either case) makes that the colour (and a recent one); anything else shows "Type a colour as #RRGGBB." and keeps the colour; leaving the field shows the colour again. Under the recent colours a Palette row (+ and Edit) and up to 24 swatches in rows of 8: + keeps the current colour at the end (grey when it is there or the palette is full), a tap on a swatch paints with it, Edit (then Done) makes taps remove swatches ("Tap a colour to remove it."). One palette (Procreate keeps many). Stored with the toolbox.
- Copy and paste (S40, D67, schema 16): three fingers swiped down on the canvas (their middle at least 48 points down, within 30 degrees of vertical, by 0.8 s) or Actions > Copy & Paste ("Three fingers down") open a pill in the top band's middle (under the islands while the selection or transform bar shows): Cut, Copy, Copy All, Duplicate, Cut & Paste, Paste, pencil grey where there is nothing for them; a command or a tap on the canvas outside closes it; a three-finger tap still redoes, two fingers still navigate. They act on the selection, or on the whole active layer with none (Copy All: on what every visible layer shows, without the paper). Copy and Copy All hold records (the layer's strokes and edits as a nested record at full opacity by Normal through its mask; Copy All a record per clipping chain and faded group), the pixels outside a selection cleared, for the app's run only (not stored, not the system clipboard). Paste puts them on a new layer "Pasted layer" above the active one, framed by the Transform tool at once; moved or not, Transform (or any other edit) commits layer and place as one step, undo at the first step takes the paste back, a committed paste deselects. Cut clears the selection's pixels (or empties the layer's paint), one step; Duplicate and Cut & Paste are one step each. Paste is grey with nothing copied or a copy from a canvas of another size; a copy of nothing says "Nothing to paste: the copy holds no paint." with OK. Everything pasted and cut reopens as left.
- Merge Down (S39, D66, schema 15): the layer footer holds, after Add Mask, an arrow onto a line and the words Merge Down. Tapped, the chosen layer goes into the one below as a nested record (its strokes, edits, mask, opacity, mode and whether it clipped, nothing baked); the lower row stays, chosen, with its name, place and switches; the canvas does not change: a layer clipped to the one below merges bit for bit at any mode, opacity and masks; two layers of one mode (Normal, Multiply, Screen) merge with at most one 8-bit level in rare pixels, the lower layer's enabled mask (and its opacity below 100 %) moving into a record of its own, so the merged row then shows 100 % and no mask. Elsewhere Merge Down is pencil grey with why in quieter small text at its row's end: Nothing below (bottom of the stack or of a group), Hidden layer, Locked layer, Modes clash (different modes, or Overlay, without clipping), Below clips, Has clips, Too deep (8 levels). Strokes, fills, adjustments, filters, smudges and transforms after a merge act on the whole merged layer; a merged layer merges again; duplicate copies it with new ids; one undo step; reopens, exports at 1x/2x/4x, versions and OpenPaint files carry it; the row's picture shows everything. While the mask row is chosen the footer shows the mask's options (choose the layer's row to merge).
- Layer actions (S38, D66, no format change): each layer row starts with a picture of its layer alone (52 x 39 points at most, the canvas's shape) on a light checker where the layer is empty, a mask row with its grey (inverted as it shows), a group row with none. Pictures are derived, never stored: the layer evaluated alone at canvas size and reduced by box means on the GPU (thin lines shown stronger: each pixel's coverage raised to its cube root), made on a worker one at a time, the topmost out of date first, only while the panel is open; a picture changes after a stroke lifts or an edit is made, never while the pen moves; another artwork shows none of the last one's. The footer's last row: Rename and Group (Ungroup) as words, the duplicate and delete icons (the move up and down buttons are gone). Duplicate: a copy right above in the same group, the same name, mode, opacity, switches, mask and content (its strokes under new ids), chosen. Rename: a dialog grown from the word, headed Rename layer, the name selected in the field "Name", Cancel and Done (grey without a name); Done or Enter renames; Cancel, back or a tap outside keep it. Moving: a finger or pen held still 0.4 s on a row lifts it (paper and an ink outline over the rows, never above the list's top), it follows the hand and the list shows the layer where it would land, the other rows sliding; let go, it lands there; the places are move up's and move down's steps (into and out of groups at their edges, half a row apart where two share a row); the list does not scroll meanwhile; a drop into a closed group opens it. Duplicate, rename and each move are one undo step; everything reopens as left.
- Library (S36, D62, D63): each gallery card has three buttons right of its title: options (three dots), duplicate, delete. Options grows a panel from the card headed by the title: Save as OpenPaint file… (".openpaint"), then Versions (newest first, each dated "5 Oct 2026 13:02" in the device's time zone, seconds added when two share a minute; with none "Kept when you leave it after a change.") and Close. "+" adds, under "From a file", Open OpenPaint file…: the system's picker on every file; a foreign file "Couldn't open the file: it is not an OpenPaint file.", a damaged one "…: it is damaged.", a newer one "…: it needs a newer OpenPaint.", unreadable "Couldn't read the file."; chosen, a new card comes first with the file's title and picture (never replacing one) and opens to exactly the canvas saved (strokes, fills, smudges, masks, groups, clipping, modes, adjustments, blurs, transforms, inserted pictures; not the toolbox, history or versions). - Ten-bit display (S37, D64, no format change): on the tablet the canvas the screen shows (at and below about 1.4 screen pixels per canvas pixel) and the sharp-zoom tiers above it hold 10 bits per channel and the window presents Rgb10a2Unorm in sRGB, which the display processor composites as it is; soft brushes, low opacities, blending and the bilinear zoom show four times finer steps (each a ten-bit code within one code of the exact value). Picking, ColorDrop, thumbnails, transform lifts, exports (8-bit sRGB), files and every canvas hash are exactly as before (each code reads back as the 8-bit value an 8-bit canvas holds). A device without ten-bit compute writes keeps 8 bits throughout. Debug builds only: `cargo device-input gradient on` covers the whole screen with a grey ramp from sRGB 100 (left) to 120 (right), its upper half rounded to 8 bits (about 21 bands, 137 px wide), its lower half at 10 bits (about 80 bands, 36 px); `gradient off` ends it.
- Export at a scale (S29, D55): Actions > Share opens with the words 1×, 2×, 4× (1× in the ink pill at every start; the pill slides) and the file's size in quieter grey (2880 × 1716 at 1× on the tablet's screen-size canvas). Save PNG… and Share PNG… write at the chosen scale; at 4× Save JPEG… and Share JPEG… are pencil grey with "Up to 2×" and taps go nowhere. Ink and pencil strokes are evaluated anew at the file's resolution (a hard edge antialiased over one file pixel; the pencil's paper at its finer level, its tone kept), and so are fill edges (S30) and transformed content (S31); inserted pictures, transforms made before S31, smudges, masks and adjustments come from their canvas data (patches and smudges bilinear). The file is rendered in bands of 512 rows and pieces at most 4096 wide, the PNG streamed band by band, the sRGB profile embedded; at 1× it is the canvas pixel for pixel.
- Smooth stroke paths (S34, D60, no format change): every stroke (brush, eraser, smudge; Ink Pen, Pencil, Soft Brush; on paint and masks, inside selections, under transform records) follows one smooth curve through the pen's samples instead of straight pieces between them; the samples Android makes up once a frame on the straight line between or past real ones are no corners. Pressure (width, flow, the pencil's contact) changes evenly along it; the pencil's graphite and the soft brush build up evenly along a fast curve. Zoomed in (2x to 8x tiers) and in 2x/4x exports a fast turn is round, not faceted; at 1x most spans draw as one straight piece (1.27 pieces a span on the author's strokes). While the pen moves, the last bit behind the tip may bend a little as the next samples arrive; the stroke left at the lift is exactly what the frame after the lift shows (the last frame, but for an end taper, S44; a smudge's last two spans follow at the lift). Old strokes show as curves too; two-sample and straight strokes are unchanged.
- Sharp zoom (S32, D58, no format change): zoomed in past about 1.4 screen pixels per canvas pixel, the canvas sharpens within a moment of the fingers standing still (0.12 s): the screen shows the canvas evaluated anew at 2x, 4x or 8x (the power of two nearest the zoom), the middle of the screen first, the edges a few frames later (a new tier on the tablet 0.4 to 0.8 s); an ink edge is about one screen pixel wide at every zoom, upright or turned; while the fingers move, what showed before is enlarged. Panning keeps the tiles held and fills the new edge in a few frames; the in-progress stroke is sharp as it is drawn, and so are undo, redo and every other edit (a large change shows the canvas's own pixels for a few frames, then sharp). At or below about 1.4 the screen shows the canvas exactly as before; picking, ColorDrop, thumbnails, transform lifts and exports are unchanged.
- Fill edges (S30, D56, no format change): a fill's edge is derived from its stored mask whenever a scene first draws it: where its pixels step along a slant or a gentle curve the edge is the line they sample, antialiased over one output pixel at 1x (slanted edge pixels partly coloured, as the ink's are), 2x and 4x; straight runs keep exact pixel edges, corners and curves tighter than about 15 px keep the pixel edge cut at 45 degrees; the inside is exactly its colour; a fill reaching the canvas's edge keeps its corners. Beside ink lines no paper shows at any scale (the flood's edge ring lies under the line). Fills on paint, inside a selection and on masks alike; a transform's lift stays hard; selection clips of strokes stay hard. The same gesture, preview, undo and reopen as S15.
- Gaussian Blur and Sharpen (S33, D59): two more rows in Adjustments. Each opens the same panel with one slider (Radius for the blur, Amount for sharpening) at 0 %, Reset, Cancel and Apply; the canvas shows each value at once (the blur softens and spreads paint, alpha too, never darkening the paper beside it; sharpening crisps edges), and the S28 rules hold: Apply (or the wand, any other edit or tool, leaving) keeps it as one step, Cancel and undo drop it, nothing changed records nothing, a selection limits it with a hard edge (the blur reads the paint just outside), on a mask it filters the grey the mask shows, hidden and locked layers refuse. 100 % blur is a standard deviation of 32 canvas pixels (1 % about a third of a pixel); from 4 px (12.5 %) the blur runs on means of 2 x 2 canvas pixels, from 8 px (25 %) on 4 x 4, enlarged smoothly (S35, D75: within 3 levels of the exact Gaussian); sharpening adds up to twice the difference from a 1-pixel blur. Strokes painted later stay sharp; the result is evaluated at canvas resolution and enlarged smoothly in 2x and 4x exports and in the sharp-zoom tiers. Stored as an adjustment (value, selection, place), reopens as applied.

## Known limits

- Painting speed (S48, D75): a stroke over a place painted on before costs what it does over an empty layer; the frames left above a 120 Hz vsync are the pen-down and the lift, which rebuild the whole scene (on the tablet 18 to 20 ms at 300 Textured Round strokes, 25 to 37 ms at 600; injected strokes of 52 samples, the author's average about 340, so his real strokes cost several times as much each; ROADMAP Candidates). The first stroke over a place after opening the artwork, an undo or a layer switch takes each tile once, as costly as a whole frame was before S48 (a few tiles a frame; a frame of the first stroke over 600 such strokes costs the dev GPU 1.2 to 3 ms against 0.06 for the next). A stroke on a mask walks its layer's paint; zoomed in past 1.4 (a view tier) a stroke evaluates every op beneath it as before; other layers are binned and evaluated as before. The snapshot takes 16 bytes per pixel of the tiles painted over plus 0.3 MB (at most 79 MB at screen size) while its layer is painted on, within the device's storage binding limit and the room the GPU memory budget leaves it (past either a frame evaluates every op).
- GPU robustness (S49, D76): what is still unbounded: a single 16-px tile listing more than 12,000 ops is one dispatch; a view tier fills up to 4 tier tiles a frame whatever their work, so zoomed in over a dense place frames run long (the 4x tier over 300 Textured Round strokes: 20.1 s over 51 frames on the tablet, every dispatch bounded); a stroke begun while tiles still wait evaluates them all in its first frame; filter kernels submit a whole filter at once. The estimate costs every op as a Textured Round span, so plain artworks fill in over more frames than their cost needs (the author's A4 artwork 1.4 s at a fresh start), and each frame bins the waiting regions again. Making the renderer again takes about 3 s on the tablet (every pipeline compiled anew), and a job running on a worker at the loss (an export, a picture) fails with its notice; the loss was tried on the tablet only by destroying the device on purpose. The painter keeps a CPU copy of every UI texture (the font atlas, gallery pictures, the reference image) for a rebuild. The budget's figures are bytes the app allocates (textures, buffers), not the driver's own; the window's buffers and the UI's textures are outside it. ROADMAP Candidates.

## Working features

- The raster core (S59 to S65, D92, no format change): the screen's canvas (at and below about 1.4 screen pixels per canvas pixel, ten bits on the tablet), the 2x, 4x and 8x tiers' tiles above it, Save/Share PNG and JPEG at 1x, 2x and 4x, picks (the eyedropper, ColorDrop's flood), the gallery picture, the display cache, transform lifts, layer pictures, brush rows, the editor's strip and the material a smudge's residual is made from are drawn by the GPU's rasterizer (each stroke's pieces and stamps as quads computing each pixel's coverage, its tiles, fills and patches onto a float layer, each smudge from a copy of what lies under it by its residual, a mask onto a target of its own, each layer's end by its mode, opacity and clipping onto a float canvas; since S65 each adjustment and filter as a pass from a copy of its layer, a transform record's content in its frame, a moved selection's or merged layer's content on a level target of its own, a group faded over a copy of what lay under it) for every artwork: on the screen-size kinds test artwork (449 strokes; Curves and Hue, Saturation, Brightness in a lasso, a Gaussian Blur and a Sharpen, a turned layer record and a moved selection, a merged layer, a faded group with a Multiply layer and a smudge) the tablet opens it complete without a display cache in 0.52 to 0.58 s (4.8 to 4.9 s evaluated before S65), sharpens at 2x, 4x and 8x in 1.1, 1.2 and 2.0 s (13.0, 23.9, 48.0) and exports it at 4x in 9.0 s (34.2); the A4 test page with 9 smudges opens complete without a display cache in 0.8 to 0.9 s (3.9 to 4.1 s evaluated before S62), sharpens at 2x and 4x in 1.0 and 0.8 s and exports at 4x in 11.9 s; the author's A4 opens complete under a new build in 1.4 s (7.5); hiding the test page's busiest layer, undoing and redoing it show complete in 0.3 to 0.4 s (1.7 to 2.8). The canvas's and the tiers' waiting tiles (256 px) are planned, encoded and submitted on a worker (`openpaint-raster`, S60): the frame loop hands up to 8 over with the smudge residuals they read present (a canvas tile into a staging slot the frame loop copies in) and draws small changes in place (an undo, a fill). The stroke being drawn, a smudge too, is the raster core's at 1x and on the tier shown, inside groups, records and merged layers too (S63, below). Since S66 (D93) it is the only render core (no interpreter, binning or cost table): a tier tile is the export at its scale bit for bit, the 1x export the canvas, and formula tests pin a flat fill, a solid stamp, each blend mode and an adjustment. The canvas's raster targets (a 16 MB float atlas, five 16 MB float targets of 1024², a group's copy and each level of moved content one more where used, a 2 MB staging texture) are one unit of the GPU budget by recency; an export's (16 MB atlas, 8 MB float targets, an 8 MB piece, its own residuals) live on its worker for the export; the 28 pipelines (S65: four for records, one more layer end) and the worker's targets are made on the worker as the app starts (0.8 to 1.0 s, nothing waits for them), the canvas's first tier atlas (44 MB) once nothing touches, so the first zoom of a run draws no frame over 15 ms.
- GPU memory (S58, S66, D85, D93, no format change): the derived GPU caches may hold a quarter of the device's memory on a GPU sharing it (the tablet: 2,831 MB of 11,323 MB, as Android's `ActivityManager.MemoryInfo.totalMem` says; logged at start), 2 GiB on one with its own. The units, and what they held after the A4 test artwork's zoom round on the tablet: the canvas (its target, the frame loop's float targets, staging and scene ops: 39 MB; never goes), each view tier's atlas (96 MB together; the one shown never goes), the raster targets filling tiles (119 MB), the snapshots under the pen (2 MB a 256-px tile: 336 MB; never while a stroke or preview draws), the arena's blocks no scene shows with the smudges' and filters' materials (83 MB; never while a smudge draws), the layer pictures (never while the panel shows). Above the budget the least recently used unit goes first (a tier by when it last showed, the raster targets by when they last drew, the snapshots by the last stroke or preview, the arena by the last smudge, the pictures by the panel's last opening), each logged. A tier the view leaves is kept whole and a tile the view leaves stays ready until a needed one wants its slot, so zooming back or panning back shows at once what was there (zoom rounds on the test artworks filled no tile again after the lift). When the app's interface is hidden (home, another app; Android's `onTrimMemory` from level 20) the tiers with the raster targets, the snapshots and the arena's unshown blocks go at once (logged: 563 of 641 MB after the A4's zoom round; the app's GPU memory 1,478 -> 836 MB); they come back as the screen needs them; the display cache on disk stays. Smudge residuals keep within a quarter of the budget (707 MB on the tablet).
- Painting at any pace (S56, D87, no format change): however fast the pen or large the brush, the app reads input between frames and never waits for a backlog of GPU work: a frame call is skipped at once while two of the screen's frames are unfinished on the GPU, and while one is for up to three calls in a row (with no hand on or over the glass until the frame before was found done a call earlier, S60), and the next frame drawn takes every sample since (none is dropped; a stroke drawn and lifted while frames skip commits whole); every wait for the GPU marks the queue and polls without waiting, 0.5 ms apart (S61: a waiting poll, in 2 ms steps before, can make wgpu-core 30.0.1 panic when another thread's poll or submit empties the queue meanwhile), so a worker's readback (the display cache's after each save) no longer holds the device's lock behind queued work while the screen presents. The pen touching down keeps the scene (it holds the next stroke's place while none is drawn) and a new scene copies each finished stroke's spans from the one before. Drawing (S63, S64, D91): the stroke being drawn is drawn by the raster core where it changes, each frame's regions (on the canvas one up to 1024², on a tier each shown tile) from snapshots of what lies under it (the float canvas and layer, the mask or clipping chain as needed, per 256-px tile, 2 MB each) and its coverage kept so far, one submission a frame; the snapshots outlive strokes (strokes appended keep them, any other change lets go those it reaches) and while nothing touches the screen the raster worker takes the missing ones ahead, the screen's middle first (0.2 to 0.8 s after an opening, an undo or a layer switch on the test artworks), so a stroke's frames take none; a frame lays the stroke in one pass of the atlas, its kept coverage read from slots per tile and the spans settled since copied back into them (S64: four render passes a frame, six before): on the tablet every brush at the toolbox's size draws its frames p50 7.8 to 8.6 ms (5 to 6.5 with the clocks up) with 5 to 7 ms of GPU (a smudge 11 to 16), event to present p50 15 to 18 ms while the clocks are low (10 to 13 with them up), p95 22 to 26 (a smudge's 38 to 54); the lift draws the committed stroke, end tapered, once, the kept scene taking it in place (S64: 0.8 to 1.9 ms, a frame of 9 to 16, median 10; a smudge or a clipped stroke builds the scene, 13 to 34 ms); S65: inside a faded group on the kinds test artwork every brush presents p50 16 to 19 ms, p95 25 to 27 (a smudge 25 and 44; 46 to 604 and 200 to 2,352 evaluated before). Every pixel is as before. No performance hint session (S68, D96: S67's changed nothing measurable on the tablet). The app logs any frame or GPU wait over 100 ms ("slow frame: …") and each run of skipped calls that long ("the GPU was behind for …"); each stroke's pen latency line says how many calls were skipped and what its slowest frames spent evaluating, building scenes and waiting (and where).
- Opening (S54, D96): an artwork opens complete in its first frame, nothing filling in, once its canvas was complete under this build: the app keeps each artwork's canvas as last shown complete (`cacheDir/canvas/<id>.bin`, the ten-bit pixels exactly, LZ4, about 8 MB for the author's A4 artwork) and shows it when it is what the artwork's records evaluate to (the save that wrote the artwork, this build's renderer, the canvas's format and size match). Otherwise (the first open under a new build, after an update, a library put back from a backup, a deleted or damaged cache) the cached canvas, or else the gallery picture enlarged, shows under the tiles while they fill in as before (1.4 s for the author's A4 artwork under S62's build, 7.5 s under S60's, 36 s under S58's), never paper; once complete it is written again, as after every save. Each save writes a new random save id into the artwork's head; an artwork without one (imported, restored as a copy, new) gets one when it is opened; a file holds none. Deleting an artwork deletes its cached canvas. Exports, files, versions, picks and the snapshots under the pen never read it.
- Navigation never waits (S53, S54, S60, D87): every frame shows at once what is held (the canvas, or a view tier enlarged) and, while a finger or the pen touches or hovers (or did within 0.5 s), gives the work still waiting at most 4 ms of the GPU's time (half a 120 Hz frame); once nothing has, every frame call, a frame drawn or one skipped while the GPU is behind, keeps the GPU's queue of that work at 10 ms beside the piece it runs, so fills run at the GPU's pace between the frames (the GPU 60 to 70 % busy with them, frames meanwhile), and the first frame of a hand arriving waits for at most the piece the GPU runs and one more; the raster worker drops the tiles it was handed the moment a hand arrives. So pinching, panning, taps and panels answer while a fresh start, a large change or a new tier fills in (the author's A4 artwork fills in 1.4 s; 7.5 s at S60, 331 s at S53). What waits fills nearest the screen's middle first, then outwards, then what lies off screen; waiting tiles show what the canvas held before (a cached canvas or the gallery picture at opening, paper only on a new canvas), and those reading a smudge wait until its residual is evaluated, a share each frame, while the rest fills. Nothing fills while a stroke is drawn and the stroke never waits; zoomed in, the tiles of the tier's ring (off screen) a stroke reaches show the enlarged canvas until they fill again after the lift. A pick, the gallery picture and exports draw everything waiting at once; layer pictures, the brush strip and brush previews draw in regions, each waited for. Every 10 s while fingers navigate or work waits the app logs `navigation, …`: frames, frame work p50/p95/max, background GPU time a frame and in all, raster regions, residuals derived, tier tiles filled.

The launcher says "OpenPaint" under an adaptive icon: the paper (250, 248, 244) with the app's Brush icon (handle, ferrule, bristles to a point) drawn in one ink line (38, 37, 35), 2 of the icon's 108 units (1.5 points on the tablet's launcher, which shows the icon's circle 54 points wide), round caps and joins, inside the circle every mask keeps; a themed launcher tints that line (the monochrome layer); vector drawables only.
- Memory over long sessions (S51, D78, no format change): many smudges no longer freeze the app. Before, about 55 default-size smudges over one place filled the 128 MiB the tablet's GPU binds for their residuals and from then on every frame failed: canvas and interface stood still. Now an evaluation holds only the smudge residuals reaching what it evaluates, whole when the device binds them all, else just their tiles near it; the residuals needed least lately go first when the arena passes a quarter of the GPU budget (707 MB on the tablet since S58, 512 MiB on a GPU with its own memory), never one the frame needs, and come back from the strokes when a later frame needs them; an evaluation of everything waiting at once (a pick, the gallery picture) is cut back to the room right after; one layer alone (pictures, fills, lifts) is evaluated in bands of 512 rows. Once a minute while a canvas is open the app logs what holds memory (strokes, GPU caches, wgpu's allocations and blocks by label, the scene and history on the heap), and the library logs fjall's cache and memtables at each new 4 MiB. On the tablet wgpu's allocator takes memory in blocks of 8 to 64 MiB (Graphics 108 MB with a blank canvas, was 174). Every pixel is as before. A place crossed by more smudges than fit whole paints and opens slowly (Known limits).
- Storage and session robustness (S50, D77, no format change): a stroke still being drawn when the app is left (home, the screen turned off, a call) or its window goes is committed as drawn so far, as its lift would commit it (every sample the app had, the line's end onto the pen, its mirrored copies, the end tapered), and saved before the app waits for the store; back in the app it is there at the zoom, place and angle left, one undo takes it back, and the pen's cancel that follows changes nothing (a pen held still 0.5 s before still snaps, QuickShape). Every write is one atomic batch: a crash, an install or a full disk in the middle of a save leaves the artwork as saved last, never half of a save; leaving writes the save, the gallery picture and the kept version each whole; a version that cannot be written is not kept (logged), the artwork unaffected; a failed save shows the notice with Retry as before. A record that cannot be read fails only its artwork's opening ("Couldn't open this artwork. It is kept as it was."; the last artwork: a new blank one with "Couldn't open the last drawing.").
- GPU robustness (S49, D76, no format change): no GPU work asks for more than about a tenth of a second at once: the raster core draws in regions of at most 1024² (an export's 1024 x 512) and 256-px tiles, and smudges in submissions of at most 2^24 nodes; on screen a large change fills in over frames within S53's frame budget. A lost GPU device (wgpu's lost callback, out of memory, a failed surface or GPU wait) is made again at the next frame: a new device and pipelines, the window's surface again from the window kept since attach, every UI texture uploaded again, the canvas and tiers evaluated anew; the artwork, history, toolbox, panels, view and the stroke being drawn stay (the stroke goes on under the pen and commits whole at the lift); a pill says "The display was reset." with OK until OK or the next stroke; at most three rebuilds a minute. A GPU memory budget (S58's, above) covers the canvas, the view tiers, the snapshots, the smudge and filter arena with its material and the layer pictures; nothing fails for it. The app logs the time, frames and raster regions to the full canvas and each new peak of the caches.

Under the words that set's brushes with their pictures, the tool's brush outlined wherever it lies (none while its set is not shown); a tap chooses, the chosen row tapped again opens its editor; StreamLine under them. At first: Sketching Pencil; Inking Ink Pen, Inker, Monoline; Painting Hard Round, Soft Brush, Flat, Opaque Paint; Texture Textured Round, Speckle; a copy lies right after its source. Recent: the brushes of the last six committed strokes of any tool (brush, eraser, smudge; not a cancelled stroke or one on the editor's strip), newest first, no repeats, never arranged; with none "Brushes you draw with appear here." in the grey hint. In the other four a finger or pen held 0.4 s on a row lifts it (paper and an ink outline, as a layer row), it follows the hand, the other rows slide to make room, and let go it lands there (where it lay: nothing, and no tap); the list does not scroll. The editor's Set row, under the name above the strip: "Set", then Sketching, Inking, Painting, Texture in two columns, the brush's in the pill; another tapped moves the brush to that set's end and Back shows that set. Sets, order and Recent are the toolbox's for every tool and artwork, reopen as left and are no undo steps; a stored key no brush has (a repeat, one past six) is dropped (logged), a brush in no stored set goes where a first start puts it (a copy whose source is gone: Painting).
- Brush editor (S46, D73): in the Brush, Smudge or Eraser picker, a tap on the chosen brush's row again turns the panel into its editor in place: the name (tapped: a Rename brush dialog, Done or Enter renames, 1 to 40 characters) and Back at the top (under the name the Set row, S47); a paper-white strip the panel's width, 160 points high, showing an S-curve drawn with the brush at the tool's size and opacity in the current colour, pressure rising to full in the middle and falling, and every pen stroke drawn on it (Clear at its corner; fingers draw nothing; Back clears it), all redrawn with the brush as set after every change (on the brushes worker, a frame or two later); then one scrolling column (a finger drags it): Stroke (Spacing 0 to 4 of the size, 0 reading "Continuous"; StreamLine; Taper start and end 0 to 6 sizes; Taper tip), Shape (Disc, Square, Ellipse, Chalk, Grainy, Splatter, Rough square as words in two columns, the stamp in the ink pill, a continuous stroke's round tip as Disc; Aspect 5 to 100 %, grey for a disc; Rotation Fixed / Direction / Azimuth; Angle -180 to 180 degrees; Rotation jitter 0 to 180; Size jitter; Scatter 0 to 4 radii; Softness), Grain (Strength, Scale 1 to 64 px), Paint (Covering / Builds up; under Builds up Tip Flat / Bell, Flow 0.05 to 16, Max density, Paper None / Sketch), Size (Minimum, Maximum: the size slider's ends, 1 to 1000 px) and Dynamics (Size dynamics and Opacity dynamics, each a page of Pressure, Speed and Tilt squares with Reset and the axis's ends; Back to the settings); at the bottom Duplicate, and Reset for a built-in (grey while it is as its file) or Delete for a copy ("Delete <name>?", strokes drawn with it stay). - Shapes and scatter (S45, D72): the brush panel lists ten brushes by job, Soft Brush followed by Textured Round, Flat, Opaque Paint and Speckle, each a row of stamps laid along the stroke and evaluated per pixel (sharp at every zoom and in exports, the same on every frame). Textured Round (size 40; size 0.35 + 0.65 p, opacity 0.25 + 0.75 p): the chalk texture every tenth of its size, turned up to 15 degrees either way at random, grain 0.3 in 5 px cells inside, covering: a chalky, broken edge, no dot rhythm. Flat (30; opacity 0.2 + 0.8 p): a square a quarter as long as wide every 0.05 of its size, held across the stroke and turning with it: one width, clean square ends, never darker where it crosses itself. Opaque Paint (40; opacity 0.3 + 0.7 p): the rough-square texture half as long as wide every 0.08, turning with the stroke, up to 4 degrees and 10 % of size at random: a solid band, a frayed, streaky edge and ragged ends. Speckle (12; opacity 0.3 + 0.7 p): discs every half its size, scattered up to 3 radii along and across, sized 1 ± 0.5, building up where they overlap (flow 2: a lone dot 86 %). On the tablet each stroke takes its own seed, so no two scatter alike; mirrored copies and a snapped shape keep their stroke's; undo, redo and reopening give it back exactly. A tap leaves one stamp. Every stroke drawn before draws as it did. Brush files may hold in `[shape]` `stamp` (disc, square, ellipse, chalk, grainy, splatter, rough-square), `aspect`, `spacing`, `scatter`, `rotation` (fixed, direction, azimuth), `angle`, `rotation_jitter`, `size_jitter`.
- Taper and tilt (S44, D71): each brush row's picture is painted by the renderer (the raster core since S62). Inker: the Ink Pen's pressure width narrowing at both ends to a tenth of its radius, over 2 diameters of its size at the start and 3 at the end (smoothstep along the path); while the pen is down the line grows from its point and its end under the pen is blunt; the frame after the lift narrows the end, and that is the stroke stored (undo, redo and reopen give it exactly). A dot (one sample) is never tapered; a stroke shorter than both tapers stays thinner than full width all along; the eraser tapers too, a smudge only at its start; the pen's outline shows the brush untapered. Pencil: unchanged up to 60 degrees of tilt (how a pen is held: the author's leans 45 at the median), then wider and lighter, at 75 degrees three times as wide with a quarter of the graphite. Monoline: one width at any pressure, full strength, StreamLine 60 % at first (every other brush 0 %). Hard Round: size 0.25 + 0.75 p and opacity 0.1 + 0.9 p of the stroke's, a hard covering edge. Ink Pen and Soft Brush draw as before; every stroke drawn before keeps its look (its recipe snapshot gains flat tilt curves and no taper). Brush files may hold `tilt` curves in `[size]` and `[opacity]` (degrees 0 to 90; a size up to 4 times), a `[taper]` (start, end, tip) and a top-level `streamline`.
- QuickShape (S42, D69, no format change): a stroke (brush, eraser or smudge) whose pen rests within 5 points for 0.5 s (by the input clock; frames keep coming while the pen is down) snaps to the shape it resembles: a line from where it began to where the pen rests, a circle's arc, an ellipse (a circle when its short axis is at least 0.9 of its long one), or a polyline of 2 to 6 straight sides with sharp corners (open, or closed as a triangle, rectangle, polygon); a stroke resembling none, or under 30 points long, stays as drawn (one try per rest). Still pressing, moving the pen turns and sizes the shape about its start (open) or middle (closed). The stored stroke is samples along the exact shape (at most 16 px and 5 degrees apart, and 0.5 and 2 px either side of each corner, so the curve through them keeps corners sharp), each carrying the pressure, tilt and time the drawn stroke had at the same share of its length (the azimuth of the nearer sample). After the lift a pill "Edit shape" in the top band's middle; tapped, the handles show (a line's ends, an arc's ends and middle, an ellipse's four axis ends: the axis turns to the hand, the other keeps its length; a polyline's corners; pen or finger, the Transform tool's marks) and the pill says Done; every drag replaces the stroke (and its copies) in the same history step, so one undo goes back to before the stroke; Done or any other intent but navigation and saving ends it. Recognition (`quickshape_fit.rs`): the path at 96 even points, the resting samples dropped; closed when its end comes within 0.2 of the size (bounding diagonal) of its start, cut where nearest, enclosing at least 0.03 size²; a line when no point strays 0.05 size from the chord and the path is at most 1.15 chords; else the closer fit (root mean square) of arc (Kasa least squares, turning 25 to 330 degrees one way) or ellipse (the enclosed area's moments) and polyline (Douglas-Peucker at 0.035 size, corners turning at least 30 degrees, sides at least 0.08 size, each side the least-squares line through its middle 70 %, corners where sides meet, the path passing within 0.03 size of each), a fit counting within 0.025 size on average and 0.08 at worst.
- Drawing Guide (S42, D69): Actions > Canvas ends with a Drawing Guide switch and Vertical, Horizontal, Quadrant, Radial as words in two columns (the one in use in the ink pill while on; a word tapped turns it on with that symmetry), and while on the hint "A finger moves the centre."; over the canvas faint lines through the centre (upright, level, both, or four 45 degrees apart) and a faint handle a finger drags (the pen never: a stroke begun at the centre draws). ## Known limits

- Painting speed (S56, S64, D91): while the clocks are low a small brush's frames come every other vsync, event to present p50 15 to 18 ms : D87 starts no frame while the one before is unfinished on the GPU, and the screen pass (the canvas's blit and the UI, about 3.3 ms of GPU at the low clock) ends about 9.5 ms after a frame begins (its CPU up to the screen's submission about 6.5 ms: the pacing poll 0.6, the UI 1, the raster drawing 2.2 to 2.8 with four render passes of about 0.3 ms each to encode, the screen's encoding and submission 1.8); with the clocks up the same frames take 5 to 6.5 ms every vsync (event to present 10 to 13). S67's performance hint session changed none of it on the tablet (removed in S68); a busy device (a sampling shell loop) does (ROADMAP Candidates). The frames' GPU clock adds 0.5 to 0.9 ms of frame work while a benchmark times them. An in-progress smudge draws the material of its new residual tiles from its layer every frame (5 to 15 ms of CPU, 11 to 26 of GPU: event to present p50 24 to 29, p95 38 to 54). The lift of a smudge or of a clipped stroke builds the commit's scene (13 to 34 ms; a smudge's inside the kinds test artwork's group 46); any other lift frame 9 to 16 ms (median 10), 1 to 8 above its stroke's drawing frames' p50 (median 1.7; the scene taking the stroke 0.8 to 1.9 ms, its ops copied on the GPU in a submission of its own). A snapshot missing when a stroke reaches its tile (a pen arriving right after a change, before the worker took it) is taken on the frame loop, a dense tile 3 to 8 ms; a stroke with mirrored copies (symmetry) lets their tiles' snapshots go every frame and takes them again there. Snapshots hold 2 MB a 256-px tile of the active place (192 MB over the pile's screen-size canvas, 304 MB over the A4 at 1x, a tier's shown tiles besides), within the room the GPU memory budget leaves them; the frame loop's targets 80 MB. The benchmarks run on the app's main thread, frames stop meanwhile. A worker's long submission on the GPU (an export's band) makes the screen's frames skip until it ends, input still read (not measured). A GPU wait polls every 0.5 ms (S61) and waits for everything submitted before it, other threads' work too.
- Display cache (S54, D87): an artwork shows complete at once only from the start after a fill under this build completed: the first open after every update (a new version or shader text), after a library put back from a backup, after the system cleared the app's cache, and of an artwork never opened since S54 fills once (1.4 s for the author's A4 artwork under S62's build) with the cached canvas or the gallery picture under the filling tiles; the file is read on the storage worker before the canvas opens (27 to 36 ms) and uploaded on the main thread (17 to 20 ms for 35 MB); after every save whose canvas is then complete the whole canvas is read back and written again (35 MB, 0.1 s on a worker, one at a time); a cached canvas evaluates its smudge residuals in the background after opening (seconds), and a stroke near a smudge before then evaluates them in its first frame; a project id a library restore reused may show another artwork's cached canvas under its filling tiles (never as complete: the save id differs).
- Frame budget and GPU robustness (S49, S53, S54, S60, D87): background work gets 4 ms of GPU a frame while the screen is touched or hovered, and while nothing touches it keeps the GPU's queue at 10 ms beside the piece running; the raster canvas's open-cold of the a4 test artwork runs frame work p95 14 to 22 ms while it fills untouched (the frame's own scene build at opening 42 ms, its slowest frame 81 to 84); a frame drawn as a hand arrives waits for the piece the GPU runs and one more (18 to 24 ms over the pile at 8x, D81's bound was 10 ms); the frame loop and the raster worker both submit, and 2x fills run frame work p95 13 to 19 ms (waits in `submit`), on the a4 test page with smudges 21 to 33 ms in two runs (waits in `present`, while nothing touches; cause not found, S61); a later tier's atlas is made on the main thread as it starts (20 to 40 ms, the view standing still), and pinches back to a kept tier run frames up to about 46 ms (S59: 50); the background measure counts estimates, so a piece the estimates undervalue keeps the queue longer. One piece above the budget still runs whole: a smudge's material tile over the busiest place takes 9 to 33 ms of GPU, so while a fresh start evaluates residuals frames run p95 17 to 27 ms (2 of 14 and 9 of 36 10-s windows in the two runs); smudge strokes over the busiest place while a tier fills ran p95 22 to 49 ms (9 to 12 with the tier complete). Exports and filter kernels are not paced; a filter's material is drawn at once as the scene is built (the kinds test artwork's opening frame 126 to 141 ms). Making the renderer again takes about 3 s on the tablet (every pipeline compiled anew), and a job running on a worker at the loss (an export, a picture) fails with its notice; the loss was tried on the tablet only by destroying the device on purpose. The painter keeps a CPU copy of every UI texture (the font atlas, gallery pictures, the reference image) for a rebuild.
